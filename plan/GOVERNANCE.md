# Meridian Pulse — Governance

**Version 1.0 · 10 September 2026**

Meridian Pulse is a public, no-login dashboard that aggregates published healthcare-policy news and US SEC filings, applies LLM-based relevance tagging, and presents ranked results. This document states what the system does with data, on what basis, and what it will not do.

---

## 1. Data scope

**In scope**

| Data | Source | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Article headlines, capped excerpts, dates, canonical URLs | Publisher RSS feeds | Published editorial content |
| SEC filing metadata and documents | SEC EDGAR public API | Public regulatory disclosure |
| Earnings-call transcripts | Bundled in the repository | Public corporate communications |
| Tag evaluations, reasoning, AI summaries | Generated by LLM | Derived, machine-generated |

**Explicitly out of scope**

- Protected health information. No patient records, clinical data, or individually identifiable health information. This is a corporate and policy intelligence tool; healthcare is its subject, not its data.
- User accounts, profiles, or authentication of any kind.
- Personal data of any category. IP addresses reaching `/chat` are hashed with a server-side salt for rate limiting, held for 24 hours as a counter, and never stored or logged in raw form.
- Cookies and analytics. No Firebase Analytics, no third-party trackers.

**HIPAA determination.** HIPAA does not apply. The system is neither a covered entity nor a business associate, and processes no PHI. It is a news aggregator whose subject matter is the health insurance sector.

**The one exposure point.** The transcript upload feature accepts user files, which are sent to a third-party LLM. Controls in §6.

---

## 2. Source policy

Sources are defined in `sources.yaml`, not in code. Each entry records name, URL, type, basis for use, item cap, and active status. Adding or removing a source is a reviewable change to that file.

**Basis for inclusion.** Publisher-operated RSS feeds offered for syndication, and public government APIs. No scraping of article pages, no paywall circumvention, no content behind authentication.

**Current sources.** STAT News, Fierce Healthcare, KFF Health News, Kaiser Health, NPR Health, CDC Newsroom, Google News topic searches (Medicare, health insurance, CMS policy, ACA), and SEC EDGAR covering UnitedHealth, Elevance, CVS Health, Humana, and Cigna.

**Conduct.** SEC EDGAR requests carry a declared User-Agent with a working contact address, per SEC fair-access requirements, and are rate limited. Google News redirect resolution issues a HEAD request to obtain the canonical URL only; publisher page content is never fetched.

**Editorial position.** Source selection reflects sector coverage, not endorsement. Inclusion of a source is not a judgement on its reliability, and the ranking is a relevance heuristic, not a credibility assessment.

**Removal.** A publisher may request removal from `sources.yaml` via the contact in §7. Requests are honoured without dispute.

---

## 3. Accuracy and AI disclosure

**What is machine-generated.** Relevance filtering, the seven tag evaluations, the reasoning text, urgency scores and labels, and SEC filing summaries are all LLM output or derived from it. Headlines, excerpts, dates, and source names come from the publisher unmodified.

**Disclosure in the interface.** Generated fields carry a visible AI-assessment marker on the card itself, not only in a footer. Every article displays its source, publication timestamp, and a link to the original.

**Known limitations, stated plainly**

- LLM tag evaluation is probabilistic and will misclassify.
- Urgency is a weighted heuristic over model-evaluated boolean tags, using configured weights. It is not a measure of newsworthiness or truth.
- SEC summaries are generated from the first ~6,000 characters of extracted filing text and may omit material later in the document.
- Prefiltering may exclude relevant articles. The fallback path caps scoring volume, so some articles remain unscored between runs.
- The dashboard shows a cache. Currency is bounded by the ingestion schedule, surfaced as a "last updated" indicator drawn from `meta/last_run`.

**Corrections.** Factual errors in source content are the publisher's to correct; the link to the original is the remedy. Errors in generated assessment can be reported via §7 and the affected item suppressed.

**Not advice.** SEC filing summaries concern publicly traded companies and are not investment advice. Nothing in the system is medical, legal, or financial advice.

## 4. Deduplication keys

Deduplication is structural: the dedupe key is the Firestore document ID, so duplicate writes are idempotent rather than checked in application code.

| Key | Document ID | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Normalized URL hash | `articles/{sha256}`, `seen/{sha256}` | Primary identity |
| Normalized title hash | `seen_title/{sha256}` | Catches syndication across sources |
| Accession number | `sec_filings/{accession}` | SEC-assigned unique identifier |
| Transcript source ID | `transcripts/{id}` | Filename or upload hash |

**URL normalization** resolves redirects to the canonical target, strips query strings and fragments, lowercases the host, and removes trailing slashes. Without this, Google News tracking parameters cause the same article to re-ingest under a new identity on every run.

**Title normalization** lowercases, strips punctuation, and trims stopwords. Google News topic searches surface the same wire stories already arriving from direct feeds under different URLs; the title key prevents both duplicate display and duplicate LLM spend.

**Why `seen` is separate from `articles`.** Articles are deleted after 14 days but feeds may resurface them indefinitely. The `seen` tombstone outlives the article and prevents re-ingestion and re-scoring.

## 5. Retention

| Data | Retained | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `articles` | 14 days | 48-hour display window plus headroom |
| `seen` | 90 days; **indefinitely if suppressed** | Prevents re-scoring; enforces takedowns |
| `seen_title` | 90 days | Syndication window |
| `sec_filings` | Indefinite | Small volume, permanent public record |
| `transcripts` (analysis only) | Indefinite | Analysis JSON only; no source file |
| `usage` counters | 90 days | Cost monitoring |
| Uploaded files | **Zero — never written to disk** | No object storage; no retention by design |
| Chat conversations | **Zero — not persisted** | Held in browser memory only |
| Hashed rate-limit keys | 24 hours | Counters, no raw IP |

Deletion runs as a step in the scheduled ingestion job. Suppressed `seen` entries are the sole exception to expiry and are never removed.

## 6. Security and privacy

**Credentials.** No secrets in source. Backend secrets live in Render environment variables; job secrets in GitHub Actions secrets. The Firebase service account is base64-encoded into a single variable and decoded at runtime. The Firebase web config and `CHAT_SHARED_SECRET` ship in the public bundle and are treated as public identifiers, not secrets — Firestore security rules are the enforcement mechanism.

**Database access.** Public read on `articles`, `sec_filings`, `transcripts`, and `meta`. Writes are denied to all clients; only the Admin SDK, running server-side, writes. `seen`, `seen_title`, and `usage` are not publicly readable.

**Logging.** Request bodies are never logged. Chat message content is never logged or persisted. Operational logs record ingestion run statistics, chat request counts, and budget events — never content, never identifiers.

**Upload handling.** Files are processed in memory and discarded when the request ends. Size and page caps apply. The interface states before submission that uploads are processed by a third-party LLM provider and that patient or personal information must not be uploaded. Disabling upload entirely on the public deployment remains a supported configuration and the safer default.

**Cost and abuse controls.** Provider-level spend cap, per-hashed-IP rate limiting, a daily token budget enforced in Firestore, explicit `max_tokens` on every call, and server-enforced caps on conversation history and article context. Every non-LLM platform in the stack hard-stops at its free quota rather than billing.

**Dependencies and transport.** Dependabot enabled. HTTPS throughout. CSP restricts `connect-src` to the backend origin and Firestore.

**Subprocessors**

| Provider | Role | Data reaching it |
|---|---|---|
| LLM provider | Scoring, summaries, chat | Article titles and excerpts, filing text, transcript text, chat messages |
| Google Firebase | Database, hosting reads | Stored article and filing metadata |
| Render | Backend hosting | Chat and transcript requests in transit |
| GitHub | Source, CI, static hosting | Source code, scheduled job execution |

**Incident response.** On suspected credential exposure: revoke at the provider first, then rotate the environment variable, then scrub and force-push. Revocation is the control; history cleanup is hygiene. Quota exhaustion is a degraded-service event, not a security event, and resolves at the next reset.

## 7. Rights and takedowns

**Basis for use.** The system stores headline, an excerpt capped at 300 characters, publication date, source name, and canonical URL. Article bodies are never stored or displayed. Every item links to the original, and the intended effect is to drive traffic to the publisher rather than substitute for it. SEC filings are US government public records.

**Excerpt discipline.** Some feeds supply full article text. The 300-character cap is applied at ingestion, so what a feed offers does not determine what is stored.

**Takedown process.** Requests go to the contact address published in the site footer. Include the URL or headline and the basis for the request.

1. The `articles` document is deleted.
2. `seen/{hash}.suppressed` is set to `true`.
3. Ingestion skips the item permanently — deletion alone would not, since the next run would re-fetch it.
4. On request, the source is deactivated in `sources.yaml`, removing it entirely.

Target response is five business days. Requests are honoured without requiring justification; this is a portfolio project, not a publication with an interest in contesting them.

**Source removal.** Any publisher may request removal from `sources.yaml` at the same address, effective from the next scheduled run.

**This project's own content.** Generated summaries, reasoning text, scores, and the application code are the project's. The underlying articles and filings are not, and no ownership is asserted over them.
